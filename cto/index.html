About Goodplace

The problem

A new home is the largest, most personal purchase people make, but the process is disjointed and forgets everything the buyer tells it. Search filters can't capture what someone really wants, so buyers spend months touring the wrong homes. Listings describe every home in the same broad strokes, so builders can't show why one is right for a particular buyer. And they spend on incentives without knowing what gets a buyer to yes.

Our solution

Goodplace is building the agentic demand index for home search. The most valuable data in housing is what people want, what they can afford and what they'd trade, and almost nobody captures it. We capture it in every conversation, use it to take the friction out of selling, and match a deep understanding of each buyer to a deep understanding of every home.

Why now

For the first time, AI can really know every buyer, just as builders can least afford to lose one. Buying a home is so personal that a whole profession exists to personalize it, and software has never come close. Now AI can hold the long, curious conversation a great agent would, with thousands of buyers at once. Whoever holds those conversations will know housing demand better than anyone.

How we're getting there

1

Start with builders

Automated lead nurture that helps builders understand every buyer. Buyers need months to work out what they want and can afford, while builders just want to sell. We bridge the two.

2

Let buyers search

Buyers join Goodplace directly or through their agent, and search across builders. Builders keep their own leads, and can share the ones they can't close to earn when those buyers buy.

3

Grow the demand index

Once a metro's homes and buyers are on Goodplace, their combined wants become a live index of demand. Builders use it to aim incentives and decide what to build next.

The problems you'd own

Agentic search for homes is still being invented. These are its five hardest problems, and we want a great engineer to own them with us.

01

Learn what people want from what they say and what they do

People are bad at saying what they want in a home until they see it. We draw it out in a long conversation, then learn from what they do next through their saves, passes, tours and private debriefs. The loop has to improve each buyer's profile and, pooled across buyers, make the next match smarter without exposing anyone.

You'd get into: preference elicitation, adaptive conjoint, hierarchical Bayes, active learning

02

Match people to homes (or, really complicated SKUs)

A new home is a dynamic plan, a lot, a menu of options, a stack of incentives and a ready date. Matching is a constraint graph and a preference graph at once, and the best answers go past filters: "this one fits if you add the $1,800 office door," or "the office faces west, and you said you work best in afternoon light."

You'd get into: constraint solving, learning to rank, knowledge graphs, grounded multimodal reasoning

03

Close the loops that make the data true

Builder sites, floor plans, photos and listing feeds arrive messy. We turn them into records where every fact traces back to its source, then close the loops that keep them right: builders confirm the facts, every buyer question gets one clear answer that's shared with everyone, and tours and outcomes feed back in. In time, that record carries a buyer from first question to contract to close.

You'd get into: document and vision extraction, data provenance, human-in-the-loop verification

04

Build an identity a buyer owns, and can share

The real asset is a person-level identity that knows what someone wants, with a search-level profile for each move they make. Buyers should be able to join their profile with a partner's or their agent's, merge what they agree on, keep what's private, and split apart again. Every share, merge and split needs clear rules about whose data is whose, and how two people's preferences combine.

You'd get into: identity and consent graphs, multi-party data models, scoped sharing, group preferences

05

Create value for everyone, inside the law

Builders are our customer, buyers have to trust us, and housing is one of the most regulated markets there is (fair housing, broker licensing, privacy). We have to help builders aim incentives at what closes a buyer without squeezing that buyer, and enforce the law where a bad prompt can't break it. And we have to create that value while staying true to ours: put buyers first, earn their trust, and protect their privacy.

You'd get into: privacy engineering, policy as code, fairness audits, mechanism design
